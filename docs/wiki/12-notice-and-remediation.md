# Notice, response and remediation

CleanApp can establish what was observed, make a versioned record public when
suitable, and send evidence through appropriate private or official channels.
It can look for changes to the underlying condition even when the responsible
party stays silent. The strategy is to make the issue easier to fix, make
persistent defects more visible to people who can act, and make an accurate
update useful to the party that supplies it.

## Repair and reporting are separate decisions

Repair becomes more attractive when a clear, reproducible issue is cheaper to
investigate and the cost of leaving it unresolved rises. Relevant costs can
include recurring harm, wasted staff time, customer loss, oversight, and legal
exposure where an applicable duty exists. Publication can matter if someone
with a legitimate stake is likely to see and use the record. A public page
with no relevant audience may have little effect. Duplicate contact and
unsubstantiated claims can cost future notices their credibility.

Reporting a fix creates a second set of costs and benefits. A party may fix
quietly because a public written answer takes time, policy review, or feels
like a concession. Increased visibility could produce more repairs and fewer
replies at the same time. Measure both.

Three mechanisms to evaluate:

1. **Use the recipient's existing workflow.** Accept a reply, redirect,
   work-order reference, official ticket, or public release note. Give the
   repair team a reproducible observation, the affected scope, and a specific
   ask. Keep CleanApp responsible for following up with the reporter.
2. **Give useful credit for a verified change.** Make corrections and
   responses available without payment. Permit a private submission and a
   carefully scoped public disposition. Show verified mitigation or
   remediation on the same record as the original concern, with a date and
   scope. Stop redundant notices once the evidence supports closure.
3. **Secure explicit commitments where they make sense.** An opt-in partner
   can agree to an acknowledgment window or status feed in a service
   agreement. Some formal procedures have their own response duties. Record
   the source and scope of the commitment. Sell dependable integrations and
   intelligence. Keep correction access and public editorial decisions
   independent of payment.

## The public record and the official route

Public posting establishes the availability of a specific version at a
given time, when the publication evidence supports it. A private case or
ticket receipt can establish delivery to a system. An issue-specific
acknowledgment by a verified team or role supplies stronger evidence of
operational reach. Preserve these facts separately, including the exact
payload, timestamp, scope, and kind of receipt that came back.

Negligence and formal notice depend on the jurisdiction, duty, facts,
recipient, and required procedure. For example, New York City's rules for
certain street and sidewalk claims specify ways that prior written notice or
an acknowledgment can satisfy the condition in § 7-201(c)(2). In a New York
premises case, the Court of Appeals considered whether the specific condition
was visible and apparent for enough time to discover and remedy it when
assessing constructive notice. An Internet timestamp is evidence about
publication. Assess legal significance separately for the specific matter.
[NYC Comptroller on § 7-201(c)(2)](https://comptroller.nyc.gov/services/for-the-public/claims/file-a-claim/)
· [Gordon v American Museum of Natural History](https://www.nycourts.gov/reporter/archives/gordon_amrican_museum.htm)

When a route carries a formal duty or evidentiary effect, identify the scope,
authorized recipient, required contents, and receipt before relying on it.
For example, Article 16 of the EU Digital Services Act specifies procedures
and receipt duties for notices of alleged illegal content to covered hosting
providers. Its scope is specific to that procedure.
[Digital Services Act, Article 16](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2022/2065/oj/eng)

Choose publication timing based on evidence, urgency, safety, privacy, and any
applicable disclosure procedure. Correct the public record when the scope or
facts change. Keep private source material in the internal operating record
or its access-controlled source. A publication history can preserve dated
corrections and redactions without exposing restricted content.

## The condition can change without a reply

| Fact to record | What can support it |
| --- | --- |
| Notice | Versioned public posting, relay acceptance, official intake receipt, or another specifically identified transport event. |
| Decision ownership | An issue-specific acknowledgment or assignment from a verified role empowered to act. Record acceptance, redirection, dispute, or another disposition. |
| Reported remediation | A recipient claim, completed work order, release note, or comparable source with matching issue and scope. An official closure supplies a disposition. |
| Independently observed condition | A comparable dated reinspection, a repeatable before-and-after test, or equivalent evidence at the same place or relevant version. Record mitigation, recurrence, and unknowns. |
| Attribution | Evidence identifying who acted, and separate evidence of CleanApp's contribution. Chronology alone leaves causation unknown. |

A complete observational record can read: “Official intake acknowledged.
Decision-owner response unobserved. Condition absent on a comparable recheck.
Actor and cause unknown.” If a behavior is intermittent or the recheck covers
a narrower scope, report the observed result with that scope. When follow-up
is missing, keep the condition unknown. Silence describes the reporting
channel and its monitored window.

## Research priorities

Keep new case studies deferred during strategy review. When testing resumes,
choose outcomes and affordable verification methods before committing to a
case. Compare channels only after the workflow can reliably record what was
received and what changed. Compare direct notice with direct notice plus
eligible publication on comparable, low-risk issues. Hold the evidence
quality, route, and follow-up effort as steady as practical. Keep timely
appropriate routing for urgent issues.

Track decision-owner reach, voluntary reply, and independently observed
remediation as separate results. Count issues that lack a usable route and
report recheck coverage. Track repeat outreach, corrections, cost per
verified handoff or check, and operator time. A rise in replies without
better verified outcomes is evidence about participation. A change after
notice establishes sequence. An estimate of CleanApp's contribution needs a
comparison or direct evidence of the role its notice played.
